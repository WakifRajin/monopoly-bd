How it works

  • The game runs on the host's device and goes straight to the others over your Wi-Fi, not through the internet.
  • Everyone needs to be online for a moment to find each other and connect.
  • If the host leaves or closes the game, the match ends for everyone.
  • Some networks, often guest or public Wi-Fi, stop devices from reaching each other. Use Play online there.

Setup, turn order and winning

  • Games on one device take 2 to 8 players, any mix of people and AI. Online and Same Wi-Fi games need at least 2 people, everyone ready, and the host starts the match.
  • Players take turns in lobby order, skipping anyone who is bankrupt.
  • Everyone starts with the lobby's starting money. The board sets the currency, place names, GO salary and the default starting money.
  • The last player who is not bankrupt wins. With a time limit, the player with the highest net worth wins when time runs out.
  • AI players come in three levels, set in the lobby. Easy plays loosely and never proposes swaps, normal plays a sound game, and hard builds and trades aggressively.

Turns and dice

  • Roll, deal with the square you land on, then end your turn. You can build, mortgage and trade before rolling as well as after.
  • Doubles give you another roll once the square is dealt with, including after buying, auctioning or passing on a property.
  • Three doubles in one turn send you straight to jail.
  • Passing GO pays the board's GO salary. Landing exactly on GO pays it too, or double with that house rule.

Spaces

  • Jail: safe when you are just visiting.
  • Free Parking: nothing happens, unless the Free Parking jackpot house rule is on.
  • Go to Jail: sends you to jail without passing GO and ends your move.
  • Tax squares: pay the printed amount to the bank.
  • Chance and Community Chest: draw the next card and do what it says.

Buying, auctions and rent

  • Landing on an unowned property, station or utility lets you buy it at the printed price. The dialog shows its title deed with every rent level.
  • If you don't buy it, you can send it to auction, or skip and leave it unsold.
  • Every player still in the game can bid. Bidding opens at 60 to 80% of the price, and the bid steps are sized to the property. You can't bid more than the cash you have. It ends when one bidder is left, or everyone passes and the property stays unsold.
  • Rent on a street is the base rent, double that when one player owns the whole colour set and it has no buildings, or the house or hotel rent on the deed.
  • Station rent grows with the number of stations the owner has. Utility rent is the dice roll times a multiplier, higher when both utilities are owned.
  • Mortgaged property collects no rent.

Building and mortgages

  • You can build once you own every street in a colour set and none of it is mortgaged. Each house costs the price on the deed.
  • Build evenly: a street can't get another house until the rest of its set has as many. After four houses, the next one is a hotel.
  • The bank has 32 houses and 12 hotels. When they run out, nobody can build until some are sold back.
  • Selling returns half of what a building cost, and must also be even. A hotel sells back for four houses; if the bank is short of houses, the missing ones are sold with it.
  • Mortgaging an asset pays you half its price. You can't mortgage a street while any street in its set has buildings.
  • Buying back a mortgage costs its value plus 10% interest.

Jail

  • You go to jail from the Go to Jail square, a jail card, or three doubles in one turn.
  • At the start of a turn in jail, roll for doubles, pay bail, or use a Get Out of Jail Free card.
  • Doubles free you and you move by that roll, with no extra roll after it.
  • After a third roll without doubles you must pay bail and move by that roll. If you can't pay, raise the money first and you are released once it's paid.
  • Bail depends on the board: 500 on most boards, 50 on Classic.

Chance and Community Chest

  • Cards move you (to GO, a named square, or the nearest station), send you to jail, pay you, fine you, charge for repairs on your buildings, or collect a gift from every player.
  • "Advance to the nearest station": if it's owned, the owner gets double the usual rent.
  • Both decks are shuffled at the start and reshuffled when they run out.
  • A Get Out of Jail Free card stays with you until you use it or trade it, and is out of the deck while you hold it.

Debt, bankruptcy and asset transfer

  • If you can't pay, a prompt asks you to raise the money by selling buildings, mortgaging or trading, or to declare bankruptcy. You can't end your turn until it's settled.
  • Once you have enough, the exact amount goes to whoever you owe and play carries on.
  • Bankruptcy to another player hands them your cash, your property and your Get Out of Jail Free cards. They pay the bank 10% interest on any mortgaged property they receive, raising it like any other debt if they have to.
  • Bankruptcy to the bank returns your property to it without buildings or mortgages, and the bank auctions each one in turn (unless auctions are off).

Trading

  • Trade at any time, on anyone's turn: properties, cash and Get Out of Jail Free cards, from both sides.
  • A street can't be traded while it, or any street in its colour set, has buildings.
  • Whoever receives a mortgaged property pays the 10% interest straight away, so a trade they couldn't afford is refused.
  • The other player accepts or declines, and you can withdraw your offer until they do. An offer left unanswered for 90 seconds is declined.
  • One offer is open at a time, and none while an auction is running. A player raising money for a debt can trade from the debt prompt.

House rules and the turn timer

  • Free Parking jackpot: taxes, fines, repair bills and bail go into a pot, and whoever lands on Free Parking takes it.
  • Rent while in jail: turn it off and players in jail don't collect rent.
  • Landing on GO: double salary for landing exactly on it.
  • Time limit: the match ends at the end of the turn in progress when time runs out, and the highest net worth wins. Paused time doesn't count.
  • Turn timer: counts down each step of your turn. When it runs out, the dice are rolled for you, the property goes to auction (or is passed on when auctions are off), a card is read, or the turn ends. It waits while you build, mortgage or trade.
  • An auction bidder who doesn't bid or pass within 45 seconds passes.

Pausing, saving and online play

  • Games on one device save themselves as you play. After a refresh or closing the tab, continue from the home screen.
  • Pause a game on one device with the pause button or the P key. The AI, the turn timer and the match clock all stop.
  • In an online match only the host can change the lobby settings. A player who leaves can hand their seat to the AI, or have their property sold at auction. A player who loses connection on their turn is handed to the AI so the game can go on.
